    {% translate "Things to keep in mind" %}

    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}These are estimates. Experts placed the parties: the parties did not place themselves, and no votes are counted.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}Each survey shows one moment. PREPPS asked in 2018 and 2019, the Global Party Survey in 2019 and CHES in 2020. Parties have changed since: Bolsonaro joined PL at the end of 2021, and PSL and DEM merged into União Brasil in 2022.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}The surveys do not always agree. They asked different experts different questions. Switch the survey to see how far a party moves: PSDB, for one, sits near the centre on the economy in two surveys and clearly right of it in PREPPS.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}Two axes leave a lot out. A party can be left on one issue and right on another, and many Brazilian parties are divided inside or change course with their leaders.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}Some places are inferred. Each survey placed only the parties it chose. Relevant parties it left out have an inferred place, marked with an asterisk (*): an estimate from another survey, which can be further off than the survey's own places, above all at the ends of the scale.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}Small parties are missing. Parties with fewer than two deputies and no senator are left out, and a few parties had few experts placing them: the tooltip says how many.{% endblocktranslate %}
    • {% blocktranslate trimmed %}The views come from Wikipedia, not the surveys. In the tooltip, the position and ideologies are how Wikipedia describes the party today.{% endblocktranslate %}

    {% blocktranslate trimmed %} Positions: PREPPS Latam, CHES Latin America and the Global Party Survey, as each survey's notes describe. Inferred places: Bolognesi, Ribeiro and Codato, a 2018 survey of Brazilian political scientists (Harvard Dataverse, CC0). Views: Wikipedia, linked from each party. Logos: Wikimedia Commons, all in the public domain. {% endblocktranslate %}
